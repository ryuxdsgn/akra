## [ ] 001 — How a refrigerator moves heat out
- hook: Your fridge doesn't make cold. It moves heat.
- angle: the refrigerant evaporates inside and condenses at the back coils
- sources: encyclopedia entry on refrigeration; a university thermodynamics explainer

## [ ] 002 — How a ballpoint pen stops leaking
- hook: One tiny ball keeps the ink inside your pen.
- angle: the ball seals the tube and rolls ink out only on paper
- sources: encyclopedia entry on the ballpoint pen; patent history of the ballpoint
- visual: high

## [ ] 003 — How a toilet refills to the same level
- hook: Your toilet tank knows exactly when to stop.
- angle: the float valve closes as the water rises
- sources: plumbing code or manufacturer guide to fill valves; a home-repair explainer from a trade association
- visual: high

## [ ] 004 — Why a pen cap has a hole
- hook: That hole in your pen cap is not for ink.
- angle: it lets air through if the cap is swallowed
- sources: manufacturer safety statement; a consumer product safety standard on caps
- visual: high

## [ ] 005 — Why golf balls have dimples
- hook: A smooth golf ball would fly half as far.
- angle: dimples make a thin turbulent layer that cuts drag
- sources: university aerodynamics explainer; a physics society article on golf ball dimples
- visual: high

## [ ] 006 — How a microwave heats food
- hook: Your microwave shakes water, it doesn't heat air.
- angle: microwaves make water molecules rotate; heat spreads from wet parts
- sources: a food-safety authority page on microwave ovens; a physics department explainer
- visual: high

## [ ] 008 — Why a plug has two different prongs

## [ ] 009 — How a thermos keeps drinks hot
