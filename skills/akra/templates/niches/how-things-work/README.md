# Starter kit: How everyday things work

A generic starting point for a channel that opens up everyday objects and shows the hidden mechanism inside. Copy the folder's files into `channels/<your-id>/`, then change the names, colors, voice and posting slots to your own. Nothing here is a finished channel: write your own angle, ideas and scripts.

## Files
| File | Copy to | Change at least |
|---|---|---|
| `channel.json` | `channels/<id>/channel.json` | `id`, `name`, `language`, `voice`, `posting_slots`, `timezone`, `started_on`, `style_approved_on` |
| `style-bible.md` | `channels/<id>/style-bible.md` | palette, typography, the host (or `not used`), opening and closing ritual |
| `ideas.md` | `channels/<id>/ideas.md` | check every source yourself, then add your own ideas until there are 30 |

## The niche in short
- **Curiosity it satisfies:** "I use this every day; what actually happens inside it?"
- **Binge loop:** one object, one hidden mechanism per video; viewers collect the "aha" and look at the next object in their own home differently.
- **Topic capacity:** several hundred (kitchen, bathroom, car, office, toys, tools, city infrastructure); estimate 300+.
- **Demand signals to check:** search suggestions for "how does a <object> work"; outlier explainer Shorts from small channels (`scripts/youtube_research.py outliers`). Record what you find in `research/patterns.md`.
- **Why it suits akra:** real objects drawn as code with a see-through cutaway; the mechanism is drawn, not filmed.

## Series formats
- **"What's inside":** the shell turns see-through and the mechanism works once.
- **"Why it's shaped like that":** one design detail (the hole in a pen cap, the dimples in a golf ball) and the reason.
- **"Used it wrong":** the common way vs the way the design intends.
- **Rooms:** a week of kitchen objects, then a week of bathroom objects.

## Hook formulas (write your own words)
- "Your <object> has a <hidden part> you never see."
- "This tiny <detail> on your <object> has a job."
- "<object> does <surprising number> every time you <action>."
- "You've been using your <object> the wrong way round."

## Scene patterns that fit
Reveal / cutaway · Scale comparison · Before → after · Myth vs fact (see `templates/shot-plan.md`).

## Sources that fit
Manufacturer technical pages, university engineering or physics explainers, encyclopedias (Britannica), standards bodies, museum collections (science and technology museums), patents (Google Patents) for how an invention is meant to work.

## Risks
- **Brands:** draw generic objects; no logos or trademarked product shapes.
- **Safety:** do not show opening live electrical, gas or pressurized devices as something to try at home; say "do not open this yourself" where it applies.
- **Accuracy:** the direction of flow, force and order of steps must match the source.
- **Repetition:** vary the setting and props from episode to episode, not only the object name.
