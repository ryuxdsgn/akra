## Ideas

## [ ] 001 — Why we procrastinate
- hook: You don't procrastinate because you're lazy.
- angle: delay is often a way to avoid a bad feeling about the task, not a time-management failure
- sources: peer-reviewed review on procrastination and emotion regulation; a university psychology department explainer
- visual: high

## [ ] 002 — Why cramming fails (spaced practice)
- hook: Studying all night is the slowest way to remember.
- angle: the same study time spread over several days is remembered longer than one block (spacing effect)
- sources: peer-reviewed review on learning techniques (spacing); APA article on effective study habits
- visual: high

## [ ] 003 — Testing yourself beats rereading
- hook: Rereading your notes feels useful. It mostly isn't.
- angle: recalling from memory strengthens it more than reading again (retrieval practice)
- sources: peer-reviewed review on retrieval practice; a university learning-science center guide
- visual: high

## [ ] 004 — The "learning styles" myth
- hook: Being a "visual learner" isn't backed by evidence.
- angle: people have preferences, but teaching to a preferred style has not been shown to improve learning
- sources: peer-reviewed review on learning styles evidence; APA article on learning styles
- visual: high

## [ ] 005 — Why the first price you see sticks
- hook: The first price you see changes what feels cheap.
- angle: anchoring: an early number pulls later judgments toward it, used in "was/now" price tags
- sources: peer-reviewed review on the anchoring effect; a large multi-lab replication project that included anchoring
- visual: high

## [ ] 006 — The left-brain / right-brain myth
- hook: You're not a "left-brain" or "right-brain" person.
- angle: the hemispheres specialize in some tasks, but people do not have a dominant side that sets personality
- sources: university neuroscience department explainer; peer-reviewed brain-imaging study on hemisphere use
- visual: high

## [ ] 007 — If-then plans for habits
- hook: One sentence makes you likelier to follow through.
- angle: "If <situation>, then I will <action>" plans (implementation intentions) help people act on goals
- sources: peer-reviewed meta-analysis on implementation intentions; a university psychology department explainer
- visual: high

## [ ] 008 — Why phone numbers come in chunks
- hook: Your memory holds more when you group things.
- angle: short-term memory is limited; grouping items into chunks lets you hold more
- sources: peer-reviewed review on working memory capacity; a university psychology course page on chunking
- visual: high

## [ ] 009 — Why sleep helps you remember
- hook: Your brain keeps studying while you sleep.
- angle: sleep after learning helps consolidate memories; a short night weakens recall the next day
- sources: national health service page on sleep; peer-reviewed review on sleep and memory consolidation
- visual: high

## [ ] 010 — Is willpower a battery? (ego depletion)
- hook: Willpower "running out" failed a big replication test.
- angle: the famous ego-depletion effect was not found in large multi-lab replications; how to read famous studies with care
- sources: large multi-lab replication report on ego depletion; APA article on replication in psychology
- visual: medium
