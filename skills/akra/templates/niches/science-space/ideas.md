## Ideas

## [ ] 001 — Why the sky is blue
- hook: Sunlight is white. So why is the sky blue?
- angle: air molecules scatter short blue wavelengths far more than red ones
- sources: NASA Space Place page on why the sky is blue; a university physics explainer on Rayleigh scattering
- visual: high

## [ ] 002 — How many Earths fit inside the Sun
- hook: Over a million Earths could fit inside the Sun.
- angle: volume grows with the cube of the width, so a wider Sun holds far more Earths than you expect
- sources: NASA Sun facts page; NASA Space Place page on the size of the Sun
- visual: high

## [ ] 003 — Why Venus is hotter than Mercury
- hook: Mercury is closer, but Venus is the hottest planet.
- angle: Venus's thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat (runaway greenhouse)
- sources: NASA Venus facts page; ESA science page on Venus
- visual: high

## [ ] 004 — A day on Venus is longer than its year
- hook: On Venus, one day lasts longer than one year.
- angle: Venus spins very slowly, so one turn takes longer than one trip around the Sun
- sources: NASA Venus facts page; an encyclopedia entry on Venus (Britannica)
- visual: high

## [ ] 005 — Why we only see one side of the Moon
- hook: The Moon spins, but it never turns its back.
- angle: tidal locking: one spin takes exactly as long as one orbit
- sources: NASA Moon facts page on tidal locking; a university astronomy explainer
- visual: high

## [ ] 006 — How hot lightning gets
- hook: Lightning is hotter than the surface of the Sun.
- angle: a bolt heats a thin channel of air in a split second, and the air expands into thunder
- sources: NOAA National Weather Service page on lightning; an encyclopedia entry on lightning
- visual: high

## [ ] 007 — What really causes the seasons
- hook: Summer isn't caused by being closer to the Sun.
- angle: Earth's tilt changes how directly sunlight hits each hemisphere
- sources: NASA Space Place page on what causes the seasons; a university astronomy explainer
- visual: high

## [ ] 008 — The sunlight on your face is minutes old
- hook: The sunlight touching you left the Sun minutes ago.
- angle: light is fast but not instant; it takes about eight minutes to cross the distance
- sources: NASA page on the Sun and light travel time; an encyclopedia entry on the speed of light
- visual: medium

## [ ] 009 — Saturn would float in water
- hook: Saturn is huge, but it's lighter than water.
- angle: Saturn's average density is lower than water's, so in a big enough bathtub it would float
- sources: NASA Saturn facts page; ESA science page on Saturn
- visual: high

## [ ] 010 — Why stars twinkle but planets don't
- hook: Stars twinkle. Planets mostly don't. Here's why.
- angle: a star is a single point of light bent by moving air; a planet is a tiny disk, so the wobbles average out
- sources: NASA Space Place or NASA science page on twinkling stars; a university astronomy explainer
- visual: high
