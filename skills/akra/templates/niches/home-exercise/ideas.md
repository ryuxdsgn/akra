## Ideas

## [ ] 001 — Sit-to-stand from a chair
- hook: Getting up from a chair is a leg workout.
- angle: slow sit-to-stand works the thigh and hip muscles you use every day; hands on the chair arms at first
- sources: NHS page on strength exercises for older people; CDC page on physical activity for older adults
- visual: high

## [ ] 002 — Seated ankle circles and toe lifts
- hook: Your ankles can work while you watch TV.
- angle: seated ankle circles and toe lifts keep the ankles moving during long sitting
- sources: NHS page on sitting exercises; a national health service page on staying active while seated
- visual: high

## [ ] 003 — Calf raises holding a chair
- hook: Hold the chair. Rise onto your toes. Slowly.
- angle: calf raises with both hands on a chair back strengthen the lower legs used for walking and balance
- sources: NHS page on strength exercises for older people; a falls-prevention page from a health authority
- visual: high

## [ ] 004 — Wall press-up
- hook: A push-up you do standing against a wall.
- angle: wall press-ups work the chest and arms with your body weight, without getting down on the floor
- sources: NHS page on strength exercises for older people; CDC page on muscle-strengthening activities
- visual: high

## [ ] 005 — Heel-to-toe walk along a wall
- hook: Walk like on a tightrope, one hand on the wall.
- angle: heel-to-toe walking trains balance; the wall stays in reach the whole time
- sources: NHS page on balance exercises for older people; CDC falls-prevention resources
- visual: high

## [ ] 006 — How much movement a week
- hook: 150 minutes a week sounds hard. Split it up.
- angle: WHO advises adults at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week; short bouts add up and some is better than none
- sources: WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour; NHS physical activity guidelines for adults
- visual: medium

## [ ] 007 — The talk test
- hook: Can you talk but not sing? That's moderate.
- angle: during moderate activity you can talk but not sing; an easy way to check your effort without gadgets
- sources: CDC page on measuring physical activity intensity; NHS page on moderate and vigorous activity
- visual: medium

## [ ] 008 — Side leg lift holding a chair
- hook: Steady hips start with this small side step.
- angle: a slow sideways leg lift holding a chair back works the hip muscles that help keep you steady
- sources: NHS page on strength exercises for older people; a falls-prevention page from a health authority
- visual: high

## [ ] 009 — Breathe out on the effort
- hook: Don't hold your breath when you push.
- angle: breathe out on the hard part of a move; holding the breath while straining raises blood pressure
- sources: a heart foundation page on strength training; NHS strength exercise guidance
- visual: medium

## [ ] 010 — Break up long sitting
- hook: Long TV evening? Stand up during the ad break.
- angle: health authorities advise breaking up long periods of sitting with light movement
- sources: WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour; NHS page on why sitting too much is bad for us
- visual: high
