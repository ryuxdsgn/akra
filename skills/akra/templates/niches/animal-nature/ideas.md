## Ideas

## [ ] 001 — Why cats' eyes shine at night
- hook: Your cat's eyes have a built-in mirror.
- angle: a reflective layer behind the retina (tapetum lucidum) bounces light back through it for a second chance to see
- sources: a university veterinary school page on the tapetum lucidum; encyclopedia entry on the cat eye
- visual: high

## [ ] 002 — How an octopus changes color
- hook: An octopus changes color with tiny skin sacs.
- angle: muscles stretch pigment sacs (chromatophores) in the skin open and shut in a fraction of a second
- sources: an aquarium education page on octopus camouflage; a natural history museum page on cephalopods
- visual: high

## [ ] 003 — How an owl turns its head so far
- hook: An owl can't move its eyes. So it turns.
- angle: owl eyes are fixed tubes, so the neck does the work with extra neck bones and protected blood vessels
- sources: a university research news page on owl neck anatomy; a natural history museum page on owls
- visual: high

## [ ] 004 — Why flamingos are pink
- hook: Flamingos aren't born pink. Their food does it.
- angle: chicks are grey; pigments (carotenoids) from algae and shrimp they eat colour the feathers
- sources: a zoo education page on flamingos; National Geographic Society page on flamingos
- visual: high

## [ ] 005 — What happens inside a chrysalis
- hook: Inside a chrysalis, the caterpillar mostly falls apart.
- angle: much of the caterpillar's body breaks down and groups of cells (imaginal discs) build the adult parts
- sources: a natural history museum page on metamorphosis; a university entomology department explainer
- visual: high

## [ ] 006 — How bees turn nectar into honey
- hook: Honey starts as watery nectar. Bees dry it.
- angle: bees add enzymes and fan their wings to evaporate water, then cap the cell with wax
- sources: a university entomology or extension page on honey bees; encyclopedia entry on honey
- visual: high

## [ ] 007 — What's really in a camel's hump
- hook: A camel's hump holds no water at all.
- angle: the hump stores fat; camels save water in other ways (kidneys, tolerating body temperature swings)
- sources: a zoo education page on camels; National Geographic Society page on camels
- visual: high

## [ ] 008 — How a tall tree lifts water
- hook: A tree lifts water to its top with no pump.
- angle: water evaporating from leaves pulls a continuous water column up thin tubes (transpiration)
- sources: a university plant biology explainer on transpiration; a botanic garden education page
- visual: high

## [ ] 009 — How a Venus flytrap knows to snap
- hook: A Venus flytrap counts before it snaps shut.
- angle: trigger hairs inside the trap must be touched twice in a short time, so raindrops don't waste a snap
- sources: a botanic garden page on carnivorous plants; a university plant biology explainer
- visual: high

## [ ] 010 — Why elephants flap their ears
- hook: An elephant's ears are its air conditioner.
- angle: thin ears full of blood vessels lose heat to the air, and flapping speeds it up
- sources: a zoo education page on elephants; a natural history museum page on elephant anatomy
- visual: high
