# Starter kit: History and origins

A generic starting point for a channel that tells where everyday things came from: objects on the table, customs we never question and words we say every day. Copy the folder's files into `channels/<your-id>/`, then change the names, colors, voice and posting slots to your own. Nothing here is a finished channel: write your own angle, ideas and scripts.

## Files
| File | Copy to | Change at least |
|---|---|---|
| `channel.json` | `channels/<id>/channel.json` | `id`, `name`, `language`, `voice`, `posting_slots`, `timezone`, `started_on`, `style_approved_on` |

## The niche in short
- **Curiosity it satisfies:** "I use this word / do this every day; who started it and why?"
- **Binge loop:** one everyday thing, one surprising origin per video; viewers start asking "where did that come from?" about everything around them.
- **Topic capacity:** very large (kitchen objects, clothes, calendar and time, words and phrases, table manners, toys, tools); estimate 500+.
- **Demand signals to check:** search suggestions for "origin of <word>" and "why do we <custom>"; outlier history Shorts from small channels (`scripts/youtube_research.py outliers`). Record what you find in `research/patterns.md`.
- **Why it suits akra:** objects morphing through time, timelines and maps are natural code animations; period characters are generic cartoons, so no archive photos are needed.

## Series formats
- **"Rewind":** today's object morphs back through its older versions along the timeline.
- **"Say it again":** one everyday word traced back through the languages it came from, drawn on a map.
- **"One story says":** a popular origin story vs what the records actually show.
- **"Calendar and clock":** why time, dates and days are arranged the way they are.

## Hook formulas (write your own words)
- "<everyday word> started as <surprising older meaning>."
- "People used to <old way> before <object> existed."
- "Everyone tells this story about <thing>. The records disagree."
- "Your <calendar/clock/object> still carries a mistake from <period>."

## Scene patterns that fit
Before → after · Myth vs fact · Reveal / cutaway · Scale comparison (for time spans) (see `templates/shot-plan.md`).

## Sources that fit
Museum collection pages and explainers (national, science, design and history museums), encyclopedias (Britannica), university history departments, etymology dictionaries (Online Etymology Dictionary, Oxford English Dictionary, Merriam-Webster word histories), national libraries and archives for dates.

## Risks
- **Real people:** never use photos, paintings or likenesses of real people. Historical figures are generic cartoon characters in period costume, named by role or name only, with no attempt at a portrait.
- **Origin myths:** many popular origins are disputed or false. Say "one story says" and source the story, then say what the records show; never present a myth as fact.
- **Sensitive history:** avoid war, religion, colonialism and ethnic conflict as topics; if an origin touches them, keep it neutral and factual, with no jokes and no sides taken.
- **Dates and firsts:** "first" and "invented by" claims are often contested; write "one of the earliest known" unless the source says otherwise.
- **Brands:** draw generic objects; no logos or trademarked product shapes, even for brand-name origins.
